# Changelog

## [1.12.0] - 2026-10-26

- The README describes the census twice in prose, and both descriptions were written while it had six fields. The feature list under "What it does" read "palette hex values, warm/cool/neutral temperature, photo regions, item counts per section, active states, button fills", and the "Census before code" principle under "How it works" gave the same six in short form. The census has eight. `CUT OFF AT EDGE` arrived at 1.1.0 and `SCROLL CUE` at 1.5.0, and neither sentence picked up either one.
- The two it left out are the two that decide the frame. Every other census line reports something inside the render; these two are the only evidence the method carries about where the reference's viewport ends, and the frame rule reads them and nothing else. `CUT OFF AT EDGE` not `none` is what builds the frame at all, and a cut at the bottom or top edge or a vertical scroll cue is what fixes its height at `844px` rather than leaving it a `min-height` floor. So a reader taking the README's summary for the contract writes a census missing the only two lines that decide whether a half-visible row stays half-visible or renders whole, which is the failure 1.2.0 and 1.4.0 were each spent on.
- Both sentences now list all eight in census order. No field changed and no rule moved: the template in `SKILL.md` and the checklist in `references/census-checklist.md` have carried all eight since 1.5.0, so what changed is that the README's own summary of the contract now matches the contract it summarizes.
- The opening paragraph's shorter description stays at four fields ("exact palette, photo regions, item counts, component states"). It reads as an illustration of what counting means rather than a list of the lines, and the two sentences corrected here are the ones that present themselves as the whole census.

Described in words, that color reaches the render from nowhere: a colorless signal leaves the choice to the render, and a role word leaves it to the reader's guess about which entry was meant. Either way the census stops being the contract the rest of the method treats it as.
- `ACTIVE STATES` now names the `PALETTE` role in parentheses after any signal that is a color, the convention `BUTTON FILLS` took at 1.10.0, and the template's stock example becomes `Home tab: filled icon (accent) + dot indicator (accent)`. A signal color matching no `PALETTE` entry is a palette omission rather than a state detail, because that line is every distinct color, so the color goes there first and the state names its role.
- The rule sits with the `BUTTON FILLS` paragraph in Step 1 rather than with Step 4, for the same reason the 1.10.0 item sat with the census fields: it is a property of the census as written and catchable before anything is rendered. Step 4's two-signal rule is unchanged - it governs the render, and this governs what the render is handed.
- The checklist's `ACTIVE STATES` item gains the role requirement and names both failures it replaces, so the audit use of that file reads the same rule the census template now teaches.
- Nothing in the README's render moved. Its `PALETTE` already carried `accent=#C9762F` and its `:root` already declared `--accent: #C9762F`, so the census now points at an entry that was always there.
- Seen and not taken: the README describes the census twice in prose, in the feature list and under "How it works", and both describe six fields - palette, temperature, photo regions, item counts, active states, button fills. The census has eight. `CUT OFF AT EDGE` and `SCROLL CUE` were added after those two sentences were written and neither picked them up, so the README's summary of the contract is missing the two lines that decide the frame.

## [1.7.0] - 2026-09-25

- The photo regions are the one part of a self-contained file that is not in the file, and nothing said so. Step 2 sends every photograph to `picsum.photos` or `i.pravatar.cc`, and Output format promised a document that "must open correctly in a browser with no other files present" - true about files, and silent about the network. Offline, or behind a proxy that blocks those hosts, every photo slot renders the browser's broken-image icon: the fake look Step 2 exists to prevent, arriving from the other side and worse than the gradient it replaced.
- Nothing in the render fixes it, so the dependency is named rather than patched. Every offline stand-in within reach is one Step 2 already rules out, and a solid block or a flat SVG shape is the gradient's failure with the gradient taken out. Step 2 now says to state on delivery which hosts the mockup pulls from, and Output format says self-contained is a claim about files and not about the network.
- New edge case for a file that will be opened offline or on a network that blocks those hosts: ask for the real photographs and embed them as data URIs, which is an ask and not a fallback, since the skill has no image of its own to embed. Where they cannot be supplied, deliver as normal and say plainly that the photo slots will be empty there.
- The delivery self-check had the same hole as the file. `PHOTOS` is the one census line the census-to-render walk can satisfy on a mockup whose photos never appear, because what it points at is the `<img>` tag and not the image - so the check now reads the tags: every counted region an `<img>` on one of the two hosts, no two general photos sharing a `random` seed, and no region quietly rendered as a gradient, a solid block or an SVG shape.
- `references/census-checklist.md` and the README FAQ carry the same two points, so the compact check, the FAQ and `SKILL.md` agree.

## [1.6.0] - 2026-09-20

- The scroll-cue branch added in 1.5.0 had no rendered instance anywhere in the repo. Both README examples carry `SCROLL CUE: none`, so the one census that builds a frame with nothing clipped - the case the 1.5.0 FAQ answer is written for - existed only as prose. New worked example: the census line, the fixed height, and the bar as a drawn element inside the frame.
- Writing it showed that `overflow-y: scroll` cannot render the cue either, for the same reason `auto` cannot. Nothing overflows: the census counted content that stops at the frame's edge. `auto` answers that with no bar, and `scroll` answers it with a track whose thumb spans the full length - and on a platform drawing overlay scrollbars, the macOS default, with nothing at all until something is scrolled. A reference whose thumb sits at a position, a third of the track long, is showing how much of the screen is below the fold, and the styled element is the only route that renders it. `overflow-y: scroll` is now scoped to a bare platform track where a full-length thumb changes nothing.
- The remedy also prescribed one axis and left the other unsaid. `overflow-y: scroll` alone leaves `overflow-x` at its initial `visible`, and CSS resolves a visible axis to `auto` when the other axis is not visible - the rule this skill already cites against `overflow-x: hidden` with `overflow-y: visible`, arriving from the other side. The frame is then free to grow a horizontal bar on the axis it exists to clip. The pair is written out now, and a drawn indicator is named as the route that keeps plain `overflow: hidden`.
- The cue's own color is a color the reference contains, so it goes on the census PALETTE line like every other. Said explicitly, because the page ground of Step 3 rule 6 is the only token that stays off it and a bar drawn by the render reads like a second one.
- The delivery self-check, `references/census-checklist.md` and the README FAQ answer carry both points, so the compact check and `SKILL.md` agree.

## [1.5.0] - 2026-09-14

- The scroll-cue edge case pointed at a census line that does not exist. It said to record a scrollbar shown with nothing clipped "in the census as a scroll cue", and the census has seven fields, none of them for one. The nearest, `CUT OFF AT EDGE`, is the wrong home: it lists items the frame clips, and the delivery check walks it asking whether each entry is still clipped in the render and whether the cut falls inside the item. A scrollbar is not an item and is not clipped, so it fails both questions by being what it is. The census now carries a `SCROLL CUE` line of its own, naming what scrolls and where the bar sits.
- A scrollbar is viewport evidence, and the frame rule was not reading it. The frame gets built when `CUT OFF AT EDGE` is not `none`, so a reference showing a bar and no clipped item got no frame and a `min-height` floor - the branch written for a full-page capture of a long screen, on the stated reason that such a capture has no viewport edge in it. A capture with a scrollbar in it has one: that is what the bar is. A vertical cue now fixes the height exactly as a bottom-edge cut does, a horizontal one leaves it a floor, and either one calls for the frame. A bar belonging to a panel inside the page fixes that panel's height and not the frame's, which is why the line names what scrolls rather than only that something does.
- `overflow-y: auto` cannot render the cue, and the skill was sending it there. `auto` shows a bar only while content overflows, and with nothing clipped the census counts content that stops at the frame's edge - so the rule produced a file whose CSS reads correctly and whose render has no scrollbar in it. The remedy is to render what the reference shows: `overflow-y: scroll` for a track that stays visible, or the styled element itself for a custom indicator. Padding the list until `auto` produces a bar is named as what it is, Step 5's padding failure with a reason attached.
- The delivery self-check and `references/census-checklist.md` now check the cue: the bar is in the render on the element and edge the census names, and no rows were added to make one appear. `overflow-y: auto` in that slot is called out as the failure that passes a CSS read.
- New FAQ answer for the case a reader actually arrives with - a reference with a scrollbar and nothing cut off - and both census examples in the README carry the new line.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-09-09

- The frame rule read the `CUT OFF AT EDGE` line without reading which edge did the cutting. Any entry on it fixed the height, on the stated reason that "the reference's bottom edge is the viewport" - but the census field has always accepted horizontal cuts, its own example is a card with its left third visible, and the edge case for carousels and wide tables sends them to that same line. A reference whose only cut is a card peeking off the right therefore got a frame fixed at 844px with `overflow: hidden`, which hides every item below that line. The counts all still match, because those items are in the markup; they are just behind the frame's edge. That is the mirror image of the failure 1.2.0 fixed, and it breaks the same rule - Step 5 requires every counted item rendered.
- The decision is now per edge. A cut at the bottom or top edge fixes the height, exactly as before. A cut at the left or right edge leaves the height a floor, for the same reason `none` does: that card says where the screen ends sideways and nothing about where it ends below. Cuts on both fix the height and clip both ways.
- The census names the edge now, since the edge is what the rule reads: `list row 4, bottom edge, top half visible`. A cut with no edge named cannot be checked.
- Wrote the mechanism as CSS that behaves, rather than as two symmetrical axes. The frame is always at the census width, because a block box does not widen to fit its content the way an auto height grows, so there is no width floor to write. The height is `height: 844px` when fixed and `min-height: 844px` when a floor - an auto height grows with its content, so `overflow: hidden` cuts nothing vertically while the floor still holds. `overflow-x: hidden` with `overflow-y: visible` is called out as the wrong reach: CSS resolves the visible axis to `auto` and the scrollbar it adds is an element the reference does not show.
- A horizontal cut also needed a row that can reach the frame's edge, which nothing said. Content in normal flow fits the width it is given: a card row shrinks its cards below their census width or wraps them onto a second line, and both render the peeking card whole - the same failure as a document free to grow, arriving sideways. The row now runs past the frame, no wrapping, each item at its census width.
- New worked example in the README: the horizontal-only case, with the census line, the `min-height` frame and the non-wrapping row, plus what a fixed height would have hidden. The delivery self-check and `references/census-checklist.md` check both directions - a frame that grows under a vertical cut, and a frame fixed in height under a horizontal one.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-09-04

A multi-stop UI gradient satisfies none of it: written inline it is raw hex in a rule, split into `--gradient-1` through `--gradient-4` it is named by value, and put on the PALETTE line as four roles it claims four things the reference does not separate. A gradient is now one role holding the whole expression, with its stops written on the census line under that role.
- Named the color the frame invented. The page ground behind a fixed frame is not in the reference - it exists because 1.2.0 centered the mockup in a browser window the reference never had - so it belongs on `:root` with a comment marking it as not from the reference, and it stays off the census PALETTE line, which reports the reference and nothing else.
- The delivery self-check now runs in both directions. It only ever walked census to render, and that direction is structurally blind to an invented color: a hex on no census line contradicts no census line. The new pass searches the file for `#` and `rgb` and traces every literal back to a census entry, a named gradient's stops, or a marked not-from-the-reference token.
- The README example was the bug in miniature, again: its body rule set `background: #E8E4DA`, a raw hex outside `:root` in a file demonstrating the rule against raw hex outside `:root`, and that color appeared nowhere in its own census. It now declares `--page-ground` with the marker comment and uses `var()`.
- `references/census-checklist.md` gained the reverse pass and the gradient-as-one-role item, so the compact check and `SKILL.md` agree.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-08-14

- Built the frame the clipping rule always assumed. Step 5 said to put a clipped item in "a container that overflows the frame", but nothing in the skill created a frame, and the default dimensions said the opposite: content height "may grow, never shrinks below 844". A document free to grow renders a half-visible row in full, which is the outcome Step 5 exists to forbid, so the two rules left no legal render.
- The census now decides whether the default height is a floor or a fixed edge. `CUT OFF AT EDGE: none` keeps the old behavior, since a full-page capture has no viewport edge and forcing one would invent a cut. Any entry on that line fixes the height, because that clipped item is the evidence that the reference's bottom edge is the viewport.
- Named the mechanism: one container at the census width and height with `overflow: hidden`, content in normal flow inside it, centered so the browser window size stops changing the mockup. `overflow-y: auto` only when the reference shows a scrollbar or the user asks for a scrollable prototype.
- Self-check and `references/census-checklist.md` now verify the frame before anything inside it - a page that scrolls instead of a frame that clips passes every count while showing the clipped item whole.
- The README example was the bug in miniature: its census listed a clipped row 4 and its body comment claimed the frame cut it, with no frame, no fixed height and no `overflow` anywhere in the file. It now builds one.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-08-08

- Added a `CUT OFF AT EDGE` census field: items the frame clips are now counted separately from the fully visible ones.
- Step 5 now separates clipped content from intentional empty space. A half-visible row is rendered clipped, never completed into a full row and never dropped - completing it invents content, dropping it turns a scrolling screen into a short one with dead space.
- New edge cases: device viewport vs an arbitrary crop (the cut only means "scrolls" in the first case), horizontally clipped carousels and wide tables, and a scrollbar with no partially visible item.
- Self-check and `references/census-checklist.md` extended to verify clipped items survive the render intact.
